# Grummon et al. (2025) front-of-package nutrition labels RCT (`grummon2025`)

This directory contains the replication data, codebook documentation, and processed evaluation outputs for the out-of-distribution consumer behavior benchmark based on Grummon et al. (2025, JAMA Network Open, 8(10), e2537389, `doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.37389`), titled "Nutrition info and other front-of-package labels and simulated food and beverage purchases: a randomized clinical trial." The companion notebook `Grummon2025.ipynb` evaluates off-the-shelf and US-ISSP fine-tuned Kev decision models (`0.8B`, `4B`, and `9B`) on this trial by comparing subgroup response distributions and ordinary least squares (OLS) treatment-effect regression coefficients against human ground truth. Detailed variable definitions, label assignment thresholds, product nutritional profiles, and survey item wordings are documented in `DATA.md`.

## Source files and study design

The raw data file `ParentFile_wide.csv` (and its Stata counterpart `ParentFile_wide.dta`) sits in this directory alongside the variable codebook `DataDictionary.docx`, the Institutional Review Board protocol in `zoi251032supp1_prod_1760453471.94614.pdf` (Supplement 1), and the survey instrument and supplemental tables in `zoi251032supp2_prod_1760453471.95614.pdf` (Supplement 2). The published article PDF sits at `../papers/Nutrition Info and Other Front-of-Package Labels and Simulated Food and Beverage Purchases.pdf`.

The randomized clinical trial enrolled $N = 5,636$ US adults who self-reported doing at least $50\%$ of their household grocery shopping. Participants were randomly assigned with equal probability across six front-of-package (FOP) nutrition labeling arms stored in `foplcond`, namely `Positive` (the status quo 1-to-3 star Guiding Stars system, which serves as the reference arm in the trial regressions), `NutritionInfo`, `HighIn`, `Positive+NutritionInfo`, `Positive+HighIn`, and `Spectrum` (a 1-to-5 star color-coded rating). After completing a simulated online grocery shopping task in a 3,926-product store scraped from Hannaford.com, participants answered 21 post-shopping survey items covering objective consumer understanding (`idbev` to `idmeal`), label noticing and use (`noticed_trial` and `rused_trial`), product healthfulness elaboration (`elaborationhealth`), negative emotional reactions (`raffect_worried` to `raffect_sad`), perceived label helpfulness, understandability, and trust (`rhelp`, `runderstand`, and `rtrust`), mandatory labeling policy support (`rsupport`), and store realism (`feltreal`, `picksimilar`, and `similarusual`). Three respondents have missing values across all post-shopping survey items, yielding $5,633$ answered participants and $114,899$ total participant-item observations across the $126$ arm-by-item combinations.

## Econometric specifications and evaluation metrics

The primary respondent-level regression benchmark replicates Row 1 of Online Supplement 2 `eTable 9` (p. 18), which estimates treatment effects on objective consumer understanding across the six pairwise product-healthfulness choice tasks (`idbev`, `idbread`, `idcereal`, `idsoup`, `idsnack`, and `idmeal`). In each task, the respondent sees two branded food or beverage products displaying the front-of-package badges assigned by their trial arm and chooses the healthier product (Option 1, coded as $1$), the less healthy product (Option 2, coded as $2$), or `They're the same` (Option 3, coded as $3$). Option 1 is always the objectively healthier product. For human respondent $i$ and item $j \in \{1, \dots, 6\}$, the binary accuracy indicator is $y_{ij}^{\text{human}} = \mathbb{1}[\text{choice}_{ij} = 1] \in \{0, 1\}$, and the respondent-level outcome is the mean proportion of healthier choices across the six items, $\bar{y}_i^{\text{human}} = \frac{1}{6}\sum_{j=1}^{6} y_{ij}^{\text{human}} \in [0, 1]$. For a decision model returning the 3-way probability vector $\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{ij} = (\hat{p}_{ij,1}, \hat{p}_{ij,2}, \hat{p}_{ij,3})$ conditioned on respondent $i$'s demographic persona in `state` and trial arm badges in `criteria`, the expected accuracy on item $j$ is $\hat{p}_{ij,1}$ and the respondent-level predicted outcome is $\hat{y}_i^{\text{model}} = \frac{1}{6}\sum_{j=1}^{6} \hat{p}_{ij,1} \in [0, 1]$.

The unadjusted treatment-effect specification regresses the respondent-level outcome $Y_i$ on the five intervention arm indicators with `Positive` as the reference category using heteroskedasticity-robust (`HC1`) standard errors:

$$Y_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{NutritionInfo}_i + \beta_2 \text{HighIn}_i + \beta_3 \text{Positive+NutritionInfo}_i + \beta_4 \text{Positive+HighIn}_i + \beta_5 \text{Spectrum}_i + \varepsilon_i.$$

In the human trial data (`ParentFile_wide.csv` and `eTable 9`), the reference arm mean is $\hat{\beta}_0 = 0.6309$, and all five alternative labeling systems significantly increase identification of healthier products relative to `Positive` ($p < .001$) across three distinct effect tiers, where `NutritionInfo` ($\hat{\beta}_1 = +0.1844$, `eTable 9` mixed-logit $\text{ADE} = +0.19$) and `Positive+NutritionInfo` ($\hat{\beta}_3 = +0.1788$, $\text{ADE} = +0.19$) produce the largest gains, `Positive+HighIn` ($\hat{\beta}_4 = +0.1194$, $\text{ADE} = +0.12$) and `Spectrum` ($\hat{\beta}_5 = +0.1083$, $\text{ADE} = +0.12$) produce intermediate gains, and `HighIn` ($\hat{\beta}_2 = +0.0541$, $\text{ADE} = +0.05$) produces the smallest gain.

The covariate-adjusted specification augments the five-arm treatment indicators with categorical fixed effects for all ten respondent persona attributes ($5,589$ complete-case respondents and $d = 44$ slope coefficients comprising $5$ trial-arm terms and $39$ demographic terms):

$$Y_i = \beta_0 + \sum_{a=1}^{5} \beta_a^{\text{arm}} D_{i,a} + \sum_{c=1}^{39} \beta_c^{\text{pers}} Z_{i,c} + \varepsilon_i.$$

Given the human ground-truth coefficient vector $\bm{\beta}^* \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and a model's estimated coefficient vector $\hat{\bm{\beta}}^m \in \mathbb{R}^d$, coefficient recovery is evaluated using mean absolute percentage error ($\text{MAPE}$, with denominator $|\beta_j^*|$ for the five non-zero treatment effects and $|\beta_j^*| + \varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon = 0.01$ when near-zero demographic terms are included), mean squared error ($\text{MSE}$), mean absolute error ($\text{MAE}$), and Spearman rank correlation ($\rho$):

$$\text{MAPE}_\varepsilon(\hat{\bm{\beta}}^m, \bm{\beta}^*) = \frac{100}{d} \sum_{j=1}^{d} \frac{|\hat{\beta}_j^m - \beta_j^*|}{|\beta_j^*| + \varepsilon}, \qquad \text{MSE}(\hat{\bm{\beta}}^m, \bm{\beta}^*) = \frac{1}{d} \sum_{j=1}^{d} (\hat{\beta}_j^m - \beta_j^*)^2, \qquad \text{MAE}(\hat{\bm{\beta}}^m, \bm{\beta}^*) = \frac{1}{d} \sum_{j=1}^{d} |\hat{\beta}_j^m - \beta_j^*|.$$

In addition to respondent-level OLS recovery, `Grummon2025.ipynb` evaluates distribution alignment across all 21 survey items at the trial-arm level (`eval_records_arm`, $126$ records with `Country: United States` in `state`) and the single-attribute subgroup level (`eval_records_k1`, all `arm` $\times$ `item` $\times$ single-attribute persona cells with $n \ge 30$) using Kullback-Leibler divergence ($\text{KL}$ in bits) and normalized 1-Wasserstein distance ($\text{W1}$) on ordinal Likert items.

## Exported test set (`grummon2025_US_long.parquet`)

`export_grummon2025_parquet()` in `code/data/grummon2025.py` exports the $114,899$ non-missing participant-item responses across the $21$ items marked `include = True` in `question_audit.csv` (the six consumer understanding choice tasks `idbev` to `idmeal` plus the $15$ post-shopping items, where `noticed_trial` keeps the raw `Yes`, `No`, and `Not sure` options and the derived binary recode `rnoticed_trial` is excluded) for $5,633$ participants across $126$ arm-item `qid`s into `grummon2025_US_long.parquet` ($28$ columns), matching the long-format schema used across `issp<year>_US_long.parquet`, `twin2k_US_long.parquet`, and `grummon2023_US_long.parquet`. Each row stores the respondent identifier (`CASEID` formatted as `grummon2025_<participantid>`, plus raw `participantid` and `arm`), the seven canonical global demographic columns mapped via `code/data/persona.py` (`c_alphan`, `SEX`, `AGE`, `EDULEVEL`, `US_INC`, `RACE`, and `HHSIZE`), the seven domain-specific shopper persona columns (`HISPANIC`, `CHILDREN`, `SNAP`, `WIC`, `NFPUSE`, `ONLINEAMT`, and `HEALTHLIT`), and self-contained question fields (`v`, `qid` formatted as `grummon2025:<arm>:<v>`, `label`, `question`, `type`, `reversed`, `option_codes`, `option_texts`, `option_descriptions`, `answer_code`, and `answer_text`). Deduplicating identical `(qid, persona)` combinations across the $114,899$ human responses yields $109,588$ unique prompts when conditioning on all thirteen persona attributes and $61,170$ unique prompts when conditioning on the six in-training `persona.py` attributes.
