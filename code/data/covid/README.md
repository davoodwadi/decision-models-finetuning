# COVID-19 vaccination conjoint and Likert benchmark

This folder contains the replication materials and documentation for the out-of-distribution conjoint and Likert regression benchmark based on Kreps et al. (2020, JAMA Network Open, 3(10), e2025594, `doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2020.25594`), titled "Factors Associated With US Adults' Likelihood of Accepting COVID-19 Vaccination." The same conjoint study serves as the primary empirical application in Wang, Zhang, and Zhang (Marketing Science, "Large Language Models for Market Research: A Data-Augmentation Approach"). The companion notebook `code/Covid.ipynb` evaluates off-the-shelf and US-ISSP fine-tuned Kev decision models (`0.8B`, `4B`, and `9B`) on this dataset by comparing regression coefficients estimated from respondent-level model predictions against human ground-truth coefficients.

## Source files and study design

The raw replication package sits in `dataverse_files/` alongside the published article PDF `kreps_2020_oi_200840_1605646100.83354.pdf`. Within `dataverse_files/`, `Kreps_etal_vax_replication_data.dta` holds the respondent-profile dataset, `kreps_etal_vax_acceptance_replication.do` provides the authors' Stata replication script for Table 2, Table 3, Figure 1, Figure 2, and Online Supplement eTables 1 to 3, and `Kreps et al JNO 2020 survey.pdf` contains the complete Qualtrics survey instrument.

The survey was fielded on July 9, 2020 via the Lucid platform using quota sampling to approximate nationally representative US adult demographics. A total of $1,971$ US adults completed the study. Each respondent evaluated $5$ paired choice sets (`pair_id` $\in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$), with $2$ hypothetical vaccine profiles per choice set (`choice_set` $\in \{11, 12, 21, 22, 31, 32, 41, 42, 51, 52\}$, where the tens digit indexes the choice set and the ones digit indexes Vaccine A as $1$ and Vaccine B as $2$). Across all $1,971$ respondents, the dataset comprises $9,855$ paired vaccine comparisons and $19,710$ individual vaccine profile evaluations.

## Conjoint vaccine attributes

Each hypothetical vaccine profile is generated by independently randomizing seven attributes (`efficacy`, `duration`, `majorside`, `minorside`, `fda`, `origin`, and `endorsed`). Table 1 of Kreps et al. (2020) and Table 1 of Wang, Zhang, and Zhang define the exact levels and wording shown to respondents.

| Attribute column | Numeric column | Attribute description | Baseline level (code 1) | Comparison levels |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `efficacy` | `efficacy_n` | Protection against severe symptoms | `50%` | `70%` (code 2), `90%` (code 3) |
| `duration` | `duration_n` | Protection duration | `1 year` | `5 years` (code 2) |
| `majorside` | `majorside_n` | Major side effects (hospitalization or death) | `1 in 10,000` | `1 in 1,000,000` (code 2) |
| `minorside` | `minorside_n` | Minor side effects (flu-like symptoms) | `1 in 10` | `1 in 30` (code 2) |
| `fda` | `fda_n` | FDA approval process | Full FDA approval and license | Emergency use authorization (EUA, code 2) |
| `origin` | `origin_n` | National origin of vaccine | `United States` | `United Kingdom` (code 2), `China` (code 3) |
| `endorsed` | `endorsed_n` | Endorsing person or organization | `President Donald Trump` | `Vice President Joe Biden` (code 2), `US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention` (code 3), `World Health Organization` (code 4) |

In the Qualtrics instrument (`Kreps et al JNO 2020 survey.pdf`, pp. 3 to 11), the full FDA approval level reads "The vaccine has been approved and licensed by the US Food and Drug Administration." The emergency use authorization level reads "The vaccine has received an emergency use authorization from the US Food and Drug Administration. This allows the expedited use of promising drugs that the FDA has found it reasonable to believe may be effective in combatting the virus."

## Survey tasks and dependent variables

Each choice set presents two questions types that correspond directly to the `choice` and `score` primitives of the Kev pointer architecture.

In the discrete choice task (`qid = "vax_choice"`, `type = "choice"`), respondents compare Vaccine A and Vaccine B side by side and answer "If you had to choose, would you choose to get Vaccine A or Vaccine B, or would you choose not to be vaccinated?" with three options (`Vaccine A`, `Vaccine B`, and `I would choose not to get either vaccine`). In `Kreps_etal_vax_replication_data.dta`, the binary outcome `vaxbin` $\in \{0, 1\}$ equals $1$ if the profile was chosen and $0$ otherwise. For a decision model returning the 3-way probability vector $\mathbf{p}_{it} = (p_{it,A}, p_{it,B}, p_{it,\text{Neither}})$ on respondent $i$'s choice set $t$, the predicted choice probability is $\hat{y}_{it,A}^{\text{choice}} = p_{it,A}$ for Vaccine A (`option_ab == 1`) and $\hat{y}_{it,B}^{\text{choice}} = p_{it,B}$ for Vaccine B (`option_ab == 2`).

In the individual vaccine evaluation task (`qid = "vax_likert"`, `type = "score"`), respondents evaluate each vaccine profile individually and answer "How likely or unlikely would you be to get the vaccine described above?" on a 7-point Likert scale (`Extremely unlikely`, `Moderately unlikely`, `Slightly unlikely`, `Neither likely nor unlikely`, `Slightly likely`, `Moderately likely`, and `Extremely likely`). In `Kreps_etal_vax_replication_data.dta`, `vaxord` $\in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7\}$ records the numeric rating from $1$ (`Extremely unlikely`) to $7$ (`Extremely likely`), and `accept_slightlyplus` $= \mathbb{1}[\text{vaxord} \ge 5] \in \{0, 1\}$ indicates whether the respondent is slightly, moderately, or extremely likely to accept the vaccine. Given the model's 7-way probability distribution $\mathbf{p}_{ij} = (p_{ij,1}, \dots, p_{ij,7})$ over the Likert levels, the predicted acceptance probability for Table 3 Models 3 and 4 is

$$\hat{y}_{ij}^{\text{accept}} = \sum_{k=5}^{7} p_{ij,k} \in [0, 1],$$

and the expected 7-point Likert score for Online Supplement eTable 3 is

$$\hat{y}_{ij}^{\text{vaxord}} = \sum_{k=1}^{7} k \, p_{ij,k} \in [1, 7].$$

## Econometric specifications and evaluation metrics

Following `kreps_etal_vax_acceptance_replication.do`, all regressions are estimated via ordinary least squares (OLS) on the $N = 19,710$ respondent-profile observations with standard errors clustered by respondent ($1,971$ clusters). The full specification (Table 3 Model 2 for `vaxbin`, Table 3 Model 4 for `accept_slightlyplus`, and eTable 3 for `vaxord`) includes $d = 26$ slope coefficients, comprising $11$ randomized vaccine attribute indicators (average marginal component effects, or AMCEs, relative to the baseline level of each attribute) and $15$ respondent persona covariates:

$$Y_{ij} = \beta_0 + \sum_{a=1}^{11} \beta_a^{\text{attr}} X_{ij,a} + \sum_{c=1}^{15} \beta_c^{\text{pers}} Z_{i,c} + \varepsilon_{ij}.$$

Let $\bm{\beta}^* \in \mathbb{R}^d$ denote the human ground-truth coefficient vector and $\hat{\bm{\beta}}^m \in \mathbb{R}^d$ denote the coefficient vector estimated when replacing the human outcome $Y_{ij}$ with decision model $m$'s respondent-level prediction $\hat{y}_{ij}^m$. Following Wang, Zhang, and Zhang (Marketing Science), we evaluate coefficient recovery across any subset of $d$ regression terms using regularized mean absolute percentage error ($\text{MAPE}_\varepsilon$), mean squared error ($\text{MSE}$), mean absolute error ($\text{MAE}$), Spearman rank correlation ($\rho$), and sign agreement rate:

$$\text{MAPE}_\varepsilon(\hat{\bm{\beta}}^m, \bm{\beta}^*) = \frac{100}{d} \sum_{j=1}^{d} \frac{|\hat{\beta}_j^m - \beta_j^*|}{|\beta_j^*| + \varepsilon}, \qquad \text{MSE}(\hat{\bm{\beta}}^m, \bm{\beta}^*) = \frac{1}{d} \sum_{j=1}^{d} (\hat{\beta}_j^m - \beta_j^*)^2, \qquad \text{MAE}(\hat{\bm{\beta}}^m, \bm{\beta}^*) = \frac{1}{d} \sum_{j=1}^{d} |\hat{\beta}_j^m - \beta_j^*|,$$

where $\varepsilon = 0.01$ for probability-scale outcomes (`vaxbin` and `accept_slightlyplus`) and $\varepsilon = 0.05$ for the 7-point Likert scale (`vaxord`), preventing division by near-zero human coefficients (such as the statistically insignificant religion indicators in Table 3).
