# COVID-19 vaccination conjoint and Likert benchmark

This folder contains the replication materials, Question Audit UI catalog, and standardized Parquet pipeline for the out-of-distribution conjoint and Likert benchmark based on Kreps et al. (2020, JAMA Network Open, 3(10), e2025594, `doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2020.25594`), titled "Factors Associated With US Adults' Likelihood of Accepting COVID-19 Vaccination." The same conjoint study serves as the primary empirical application in Wang, Zhang, and Zhang (Marketing Science, "Large Language Models for Market Research: A Data-Augmentation Approach"). Within this package, `stimulus.py` builds the complete Qualtrics survey flow catalog and audit artifacts (`question_audit.csv`, `question_tags.json`, and `.audit_details.json`), while `__init__.py` (`data.covid`) exports `covid_US_long.parquet`, loads Kev evaluation views (`load_covid_eval`), and estimates respondent-clustered AMCEs (`evaluate_covid_ols`).

## Source files and study design

The raw replication package sits in `dataverse_files/` alongside the published article PDF `kreps_2020_oi_200840_1605646100.83354.pdf`. Within `dataverse_files/`, `Kreps_etal_vax_replication_data.dta` holds the respondent-profile dataset, `kreps_etal_vax_acceptance_replication.do` provides the authors' Stata replication script for Table 2, Table 3, Figure 1, Figure 2, and Online Supplement eTables 1 to 3, and `Kreps et al JNO 2020 survey.pdf` contains the complete Qualtrics survey instrument.

The survey was fielded on July 9, 2020 via the Lucid platform using quota sampling to approximate nationally representative US adult demographics. A total of $1,971$ US adults completed the study. Each respondent evaluated $5$ paired choice sets (`pair_id` $\in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$), with $2$ hypothetical vaccine profiles per choice set (`choice_set` $\in \{11, 12, 21, 22, 31, 32, 41, 42, 51, 52\}$, where the tens digit indexes the choice set and the ones digit indexes Vaccine A as $1$ and Vaccine B as $2$). Across all $1,971$ respondents, the dataset comprises $9,855$ paired vaccine comparisons and $19,710$ individual vaccine profile evaluations.

## Conjoint vaccine attributes

Each hypothetical vaccine profile is generated by independently randomizing seven attributes (`efficacy`, `duration`, `majorside`, `minorside`, `fda`, `origin`, and `endorsed`). Table 1 of Kreps et al. (2020) and Table 1 of Wang, Zhang, and Zhang define the exact levels and wording shown to respondents.

| Attribute column | Numeric column | Attribute description | Baseline level (code 1) | Comparison levels |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `efficacy` | `efficacy_n` | Protection against severe symptoms | `50%` | `70%` (code 2), `90%` (code 3) |
| `duration` | `duration_n` | Protection duration | `1 year` | `5 years` (code 2) |
| `majorside` | `majorside_n` | Major side effects (hospitalization or death) | `1 in 10,000` | `1 in 1,000,000` (code 2) |
| `minorside` | `minorside_n` | Minor side effects (flu-like symptoms) | `1 in 10` | `1 in 30` (code 2) |
| `fda` | `fda_n` | FDA approval process | Full FDA approval and license | Emergency use authorization (EUA, code 2) |
| `origin` | `origin_n` | National origin of vaccine | `United States` | `United Kingdom` (code 2), `China` (code 3) |
| `endorsed` | `endorsed_n` | Endorsing person or organization | `President Donald Trump` | `Vice President Joe Biden` (code 2), `US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention` (code 3), `World Health Organization` (code 4) |

In the Qualtrics instrument (`Kreps et al JNO 2020 survey.pdf`, pp. 3 to 11), the full FDA approval level reads "The vaccine has been approved and licensed by the US Food and Drug Administration." The emergency use authorization level reads "The vaccine has received an emergency use authorization from the US Food and Drug Administration. This allows the expedited use of promising drugs that the FDA has found it reasonable to believe may be effective in combatting the virus."

## Respondent persona attributes and post-conjoint questions

After completing the five conjoint choice tasks, each participant answered demographic, political, and COVID-19 attitudinal questions (`Kreps et al JNO 2020 survey.pdf`, pp. 11 to 17). Respondent background attributes are mapped strictly through `code/data/persona.py` (`DATASET_PERSONA_COLS["covid"]`), while the thirteen post-conjoint COVID-19, health, and political items (`work_status`, `work_home`, `personal_contact1`, `personal_contact2`, `covid_future`, `covid_fed_approve`, `covid_china`, `flu_vaccine`, `vaccine_safety`, `mandatory_vax`, `insurance`, `pharma`, and `pres_approval`) are cataloged as auditable questions in `question_audit.csv` rather than injected into `state`. Two demographic follow-up items in the Qualtrics `Demographics` block are intentionally excluded from both `persona.py` and substantive questions (`0_Demographics` with `include = False`). First, because `party_lean` was administered without skip logic to self-identified partisans as well as independents, `US_PRTY` uses `party3` exclusively (`1` to `Republican`, `2` to `Democrat`, `3` to `Independent`, and `4` to `Something else`), leaving `party_lean` excluded. Second, `evangelical` was administered conditionally only when `relig` fell in `{1, 2, 3, 4}` (`Protestant`, `Roman Catholic`, `Mormon`, or `Orthodox`, with `985` missing non-Christian respondents); because neither `issp` nor `twin2k` (`QID18`) includes a born-again persona attribute and folding `evangelical` into `Other Christian` would overwrite `Protestant`, `Catholic`, and `Orthodox` affiliations, `RELIGGRP` uses `relig` alone and `evangelical` remains excluded. For the select-all-that-apply race items (`race_1` through `race_6`), respondents checking a single category map to that category (`White`, `Black`, `Hispanic`, `Asian`, or `Other` for `American Indian` and `Other`), whereas multi-racial selections (`len(checked) > 1`) map to `Other`.

## Survey tasks and dependent variables

Each choice set presents two question types that correspond directly to the `choice` and `score` primitives of the Kev pointer architecture, with $576$ distinct full-factorial profiles (`p001` through `p576`) across the seven randomized vaccine attributes.

In the discrete choice task (`v = "vax_choice"`, `qid = "covid:vax_choice:<pA>_<pB>"`, `type = "choice"`), respondents compare Vaccine A and Vaccine B side by side and answer "If you had to choose, would you choose to get Vaccine A or Vaccine B, or would you choose not to be vaccinated?" with three options (`Vaccine A`, `Vaccine B`, and `I would choose not to get either vaccine`, coded `1`, `2`, and `3`, where each vaccine's seven attributes are attached in `option_descriptions`). In `Kreps_etal_vax_replication_data.dta`, the binary outcome `vaxbin` $\in \{0, 1\}$ equals $1$ if the profile was chosen and $0$ otherwise. For a decision model returning the 3-way probability vector $\mathbf{p}_{it} = (p_{it,A}, p_{it,B}, p_{it,\text{Neither}})$ on respondent $i$'s choice set $t$, the predicted choice probability is $\hat{y}_{it,A}^{\text{choice}} = p_{it,A}$ for Vaccine A (`option_ab == 1`) and $\hat{y}_{it,B}^{\text{choice}} = p_{it,B}$ for Vaccine B (`option_ab == 2`).

In the individual vaccine evaluation task (`v = "vax_likert"`, `qid = "covid:vax_likert:<pID>"`, `type = "score"`, `reversed = True`), respondents evaluate each vaccine profile individually and answer "How likely or unlikely would you be to get the vaccine described above?" on the verbatim Qualtrics descending 7-point Likert scale (`Extremely likely`, `Moderately likely`, `Slightly likely`, `Neither likely nor unlikely`, `Slightly unlikely`, `Moderately unlikely`, and `Extremely unlikely`, with `option_codes = [7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1]`). In `Kreps_etal_vax_replication_data.dta`, `vaxord` $\in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7\}$ records the numeric rating from $1$ (`Extremely unlikely`) to $7$ (`Extremely likely`), and `vaccine_accept` $= \mathbb{1}[\text{vaxord} \ge 5] \in \{0, 1\}$ indicates whether the respondent is slightly, moderately, or extremely likely to accept the vaccine. Given the model's 7-way probability distribution $\mathbf{p}_{ij} = (p_{ij,0}, \dots, p_{ij,6})$ ordered from `Extremely likely` (index $0$, code $7$) to `Extremely unlikely` (index $6$, code $1$), the predicted acceptance probability for Table 3 and Figure 1 is

$$\hat{y}_{ij}^{\text{accept}} = \sum_{m=0}^{2} p_{ij,m} \in [0, 1],$$

and the expected 7-point Likert score is

$$\hat{y}_{ij}^{\text{vaxord}} = \sum_{m=0}^{6} (7 - m) \, p_{ij,m} \in [1, 7].$$

## Econometric specifications and evaluation metrics

Following Kreps et al. (2020, Table 3 Models 1 and 3 and Figure 1), `evaluate_covid_ols` estimates average marginal component effects (AMCEs) via ordinary least squares (OLS) on the $N = 19,710$ respondent-profile observations with standard errors clustered by respondent ($1,971$ clusters) across the $d = 11$ randomized vaccine attribute indicators relative to each attribute's baseline level:

$$Y_{ij} = \beta_0 + \sum_{a=1}^{11} \beta_a^{\text{attr}} X_{ij,a} + \varepsilon_{ij}.$$

Let $\bm{\beta}^* \in \mathbb{R}^{11}$ denote the human ground-truth AMCE vector and $\hat{\bm{\beta}}^m \in \mathbb{R}^{11}$ denote the AMCE vector estimated when replacing the human outcome $Y_{ij}$ (`vaxbin` for discrete choice and `vaccine_accept` for Likert acceptance) with decision model $m$'s respondent-level prediction $\hat{y}_{ij}^m$. Coefficient recovery is reported separately for choice, Likert acceptance, and pooled effects (`22` terms) using mean absolute percentage error ($\text{MAPE}$), mean absolute error ($\text{MAE}$), and Spearman rank correlation ($\rho$):

$$\text{MAPE}(\hat{\bm{\beta}}^m, \bm{\beta}^*) = \frac{100}{d} \sum_{j=1}^{d} \frac{|\hat{\beta}_j^m - \beta_j^*|}{|\beta_j^*|}, \qquad \text{MAE}(\hat{\bm{\beta}}^m, \bm{\beta}^*) = \frac{1}{d} \sum_{j=1}^{d} |\hat{\beta}_j^m - \beta_j^*|.$$
