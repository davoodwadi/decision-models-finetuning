# Kantorowicz 2026 decision log

This document records the source files, persona mapping choices, audit catalog entries, and export design for Kantorowicz-Reznichenko, van Heusden, and Kantorowicz (2026), published in the Journal of Law and Empirical Analysis (vol. 3, no. 1). The study fielded paired conjoint experiments on consumer valuation of contractual rights across laptop computers and laundry machines in October 2023 on a quota-representative United States sample via MSI/Savanta.

## Source files

The Dataverse replication archive in `dataverse_files/` provides five main files. The `computers.RDS` table contains 26,842 rows across 2,238 respondents (13,421 paired choice tasks), and `laundry_machines.RDS` contains 26,804 rows across 2,239 respondents (13,402 paired choice tasks), yielding 2,242 unique `Response.ID` values in total with 2,235 respondents completing both products. In both tables, the first half of rows holds Option A and the second half holds Option B, with exact `Response.ID` alignment and `selected[i] + selected[i + h] == 1` verified across 100 percent of rows including incomplete respondents (six in computers with four or five tasks, thirteen in laundry machines with one to five tasks). The files `amces_computers.csv`, `amces_laundry_machines.csv`, and `script_final.R` store the published respondent-clustered OLS AMCE estimates computed with `cregg::amce` (19 non-reference AMCEs per product, 38 total). The file `manipulation_checks.RDS` holds 3,547 rows across 12 legal-term comprehension sliders (1 to 7 scale) without `Response.ID` or demographic columns. Because `pyreadr` is not installed in the workspace environment, `stimulus.py` reads the `.RDS` tables through `Rscript` with exact string preservation and converts only R `NA` values to `NaN`.

## Persona mapping

All respondent-level columns were verified to be 100 percent constant within each `Response.ID` and 100 percent identical across both products for all 2,235 shared respondents. Every decision below is implemented in `code/data/persona.py` under dataset key `kantorowicz2026`, and missing values (`NaN`) are omitted from the respondent persona string by `base.py`.

**Country (`c_alphan`).** Every respondent is assigned `United States`, because the study sampled a quota-representative American adult sample through MSI/Savanta (N = 2,242).

**Sex (`SEX`).** Mapped directly from `gender_dummy` to existing global levels `Female` (1,127) and `Male` (1,084), with 31 missing values mapped to `NaN`.

**Age (`AGE`).** Mapped from `age_dummy` to two new global levels, `Older` (1,148) and `Younger` (1,074), with 20 missing values mapped to `NaN`. Because no numeric age cutoff is reported in the paper, supplement, or replication script, the labels are kept verbatim rather than coerced into numeric age brackets.

**Education (`EDULEVEL`).** Mapped from `education_dummy` to two new global levels that expand the raw abbreviation into natural English, with `Below higher edu.` mapped to `Below higher education` (1,215), `Higher edu.` mapped to `Higher education` (1,025), and 2 missing values mapped to `NaN`. No exact degree threshold is documented in the paper or supplement.

**Income (`US_INC`).** Mapped from `income_dummy` to two new global levels, `Higher income` (1,324) and `Lower income` (789), with 129 missing values mapped to `NaN`, because no dollar cutoff is documented in the paper or supplement.

**Excluded individual columns.** Three respondent-level non-demographic columns are excluded from both the persona and the exported questions: `risk_aversion_dummy` (1,362 `Risk averse`, 381 `Risk seeking`, 499 missing) is an undocumented behavioral task unused in the paper or replication script; `analytical_thinking_dummy` (1,411 `No`, 821 `Yes`, 10 missing) is a binarized score from the three-item Cognitive Reflection Test with an undocumented cutoff and no raw item responses in the archive; and `consistent_switch` (1,255 zero, 987 one) is the undocumented switching consistency flag paired with the risk task. Two brand-level derived columns are also excluded: `Brand_Familiarity` is a deterministic recode of the conjoint `Brand` attribute, and `trust_dummy` is a binarized post-conjoint brand reliability index that varies by brand within each respondent without raw item scores in the archive.

## Audit catalog and stimulus wording

The audit catalog in `question_audit.csv` exposes both conjoint choice tasks (`computer_choice` and `laundry_choice`, initially marked `include=True` with `kev_type=choice`) alongside all nine excluded demographic, trait, and brand columns plus the unlinked `manipulation_checks.RDS` table so that every classification can be reviewed in the Next.js audit application.

**Prompt wording and profile formatting.** Question stems, option names, attribute labels, and participant-facing level text follow Figure 1 (p. 114) and Tables 1 and 2 (p. 113) of the published paper verbatim, including product-specific punctuation on the Liability row where laptop computers end with a question mark and laundry machines omit it. Computers use the stem "Which of the laptop computers (A or B) would you prefer to buy?" with options `Laptop computer A` and `Laptop computer B`; laundry machines use "Which of the laundry machines (A or B) would you prefer to buy?" with options `Laundry machine A` and `Laundry machine B`. Shortened `.RDS` labels for Privacy are restored to their Figure 1 on-screen wording (`No privacy` to `Collected, processed and stored`, `No privacy: marketing` to `Collected and processed for marketing purposes`, `No privacy: sustainability` to `Collected and processed for the development of more sustainable products`, and `Full privacy` to `Not collected nor processed`), and leading whitespace in `Conflict.Resolution` (computers) and `Liability` (laundry machines) is stripped. Legal-right row explanations from Figure 1 are placed in parentheses on the attribute label, e.g., `Privacy (what will be done with your personal data which you provide in the process of purchase?)`, to avoid double colons when serializing table rows into inline key-value option descriptions, matching the convention used in Kreps et al. (2020). Because per-respondent randomized attribute row order is not stored in the `.RDS` files, inline descriptions follow the canonical Table 1 and Table 2 order.
