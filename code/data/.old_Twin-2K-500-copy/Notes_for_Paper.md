# Notes on Twin-2K-500 for paper writing

## Overview and demographic alignment

Twin-2K-500 (Toubia et al., 2025) surveys $N = 2,058$ US participants on Prolific across four waves, where Waves 1 to 3 collect demographics, personality traits, cognitive performance, economic preferences, and behavioral experiments, and Wave 4 repeats the behavioral economics and pricing tasks two weeks later to measure human test-retest reliability. Of the 14 demographic variables (`QID11` to `QID24`), 12 map directly onto our 15 ISSP US persona attributes (with `AGE` using the exact same four brackets of `18-29`, `30-49`, `50-64`, and `65+`). The dataset does not include `VOTE_LE`, `URBRURAL`, or `UNION` from ISSP, and adds US citizenship (`QID16`) and household size (`QID23`).

## Mixture of subjective preferences and objective cognitive tasks

Unlike standard social surveys that focus exclusively on subjective attitudes and self-reports, Twin-2K-500 combines preference and personality items with objective cognitive and general-knowledge tests, including vocabulary synonyms and antonyms, formal syllogisms, financial literacy, and text-described visual matrix and cube rotation puzzles. In our distributional modeling framework, these cognitive items remain valuable training and evaluation targets because the human responses capture empirical accuracy rates and systematic bounded-rationality distractors across demographic groups (e.g., variation by education and age) rather than a single deterministic answer.

## Multi-select checkboxes versus mutually exclusive softmax outputs

While 154 of the 175 multiple-choice catalog entries use standard single-answer radio buttons (`SAVR` and `SAHR`), 21 entries use multi-select checkboxes (`MAVR` and `MAHR`), which expand into 84 binary indicator columns in the CSV files. One of these is the Wason four-card selection task (`QID221`), where logical falsification of the rule requires turning over two cards (`A` and `7`, selected together by only 4.6% of respondents, whereas 52.2% select the confirmation-bias pair `A` and `3` and 29.6% select `A` alone). The other 20 multi-select entries are the Beck Depression Inventory (BDI) items (`QID126` to `QID147`), where instructions allowed participants to check multiple severity statements if several applied equally well. Because a decision model produces a softmax distribution over mutually exclusive options summing to one, multi-select items cannot be fed directly as four-option softmax questions without reformulation. Depending on our design choice, they can be excluded, decomposed into four separate binary questions per option column, modeled over the power set of selected card combinations (for `QID221`), or reduced to a single four-level ordinal severity score for the BDI items (where over 91% of respondents selected only a single statement).

## Incomplete follow-up stems and empty question text

Several sequential tasks in the survey store the shared scenario setup only in the lead question or in a preceding descriptive block (`DB`), leaving follow-up catalog entries without self-contained instructions. In the cognitive block, the conditional logic questions (`QID268` to `QID270`) omit the premise stated in `QID278` (*"Suppose that it is true that: All the XAR's are YOF's"*). In the economic preferences block, the ultimatum game receiver items (`QID226` to `QID230`) and trust game receiver items (`QID119` to `QID122`) contain only the conditional offer clause (e.g., *"If the person offers to keep $1 for themselves and send me $4:"*) and rely on the game rules introduced in `QID224` to `QID225` and `QID117` to `QID118`. Similarly, all 20 BDI items (`QID126` to `QID147`) have empty `QuestionText` fields in the catalog and rely on the preceding descriptive blocks (`QID127` and `QID240`). Constructing standalone prompts for a decision model requires prepending the lead setup stem to each follow-up item.

## Likert matrix expansion and vignette separation

The 23 Likert matrix batteries (`QuestionType == "Matrix"`, `Selector == "Likert"`) expand into 290 CSV columns (`{QID}_{RowID}`), where each `csv_columns[i]` corresponds to a single sub-question `Rows[i]` answered on the shared scale `Columns`. For our decision models, each row becomes an individual question whose `instructions` field concatenates the matrix stem `QuestionText` with `Rows[i]`, while `state` always represents the actual survey respondent's demographic persona (`QID11` to `QID24`). When a question contains a hypothetical third-person vignette (such as the description of Linda in `QID159` and `QID160`, Jack in `QID154` and `QID156`, or Mr. A and Mr. B in `QID34`), that vignette belongs strictly to `instructions` as the stimulus being judged by the respondent, never to `state`. Because Qualtrics labels any standard single-answer matrix table as `"Selector": "Likert"`, we assign the decision question type using `q_type = "score" if len(q["Columns"]) > 2 else "choice"`. This rule cleanly maps the 20 multi-point rating batteries (261 columns, including the three partially labeled numeric scales `QID29`, `QID30`, and `QID125` and the `QID233` `"Agree strongy"` spelling preserved in both catalog and CSV) to `score`, and maps the three 2-column binary matrices (`QID27` with `['FALSE', 'TRUE']`, `QID203` with `['red', 'green']`, and `QID198` with `['1', '2']`, totaling 29 columns) to `choice`.

## Bipolar multiple price lists encoded in row strings

The 13 economic preference multiple price list batteries (`QuestionType == "Matrix"`, `Selector == "Bipolar"`, 158 CSV columns across 6 time-discounting batteries and 7 risk or loss-aversion lottery batteries) elicit preferences through a sequence of binary trade-offs. In the catalog, `Columns` is `['1', '2']` while the actual pair of alternatives for each row is concatenated inside `Rows[i]` with a colon separator (e.g., `$6.00 in 6 weeks:$3.00 in 5 weeks` or `Lottery:$2.50`). For our decision models, each row becomes an individual binary `choice` question that retains the original `QuestionText` verbatim as `instructions` (preserving the exact human survey wording and training the model to handle real-world survey instructions directly) and splits `Rows[i]` on the colon into the two option labels `opt1` and `opt2` (`criteria = {opt1: None, opt2: None}`), mapping response `'1'` to `opt1` and `'2'` to `opt2`.

## Respondent-specific prices and between-subject experimental conditions

In the 40-item product pricing study (`QID9_1` to `QID9_40`), each participant saw a randomly drawn price for each grocery product, and kept that same respondent-specific price in Wave 4. However, `question_catalog.json` stores the `QuestionText` (and thus the price) from only a single participant, while the CSV files record only the binary Yes/No purchase response. Using the pricing study at the respondent or price-conditioned level requires extracting each participant's actual question text from the `wave_split` parquet files rather than using the catalog text. In addition, the between-subject behavioral economics experiments (such as high versus low anchoring, gain versus loss framing, and between-subject proportion dominance) randomly assign each participant to a single experimental arm, so each condition-specific CSV column is observed for roughly half the sample ($n \approx 1,000$ to $1,050$) and `NaN` for the remaining participants.

## Excluded question types (sliders, open text entries, and descriptive blocks) and duplicate question IDs

Out of the 760 response columns, we exclude the 12 columns from continuous 0 to 100 horizontal sliders (`QuestionType == "Slider"`, covering the two base-rate engineer items `QID154` and `QID156` and the 10 perceived-public-support items `QID290_1` to `QID290_12`, where respondents entered 81 to 100 distinct integer values per item) and the 62 columns from open numeric or free-text entries (`QuestionType == "TE"`, such as anchoring numeric estimates, Cognitive Reflection Test number entries, and 20-word Forward Flow associations), along with the 14 descriptive instruction blocks (`QuestionType == "DB"`, 0 CSV columns). Excluding continuous sliders and free-text entries avoids synthesizing artificial bin boundaries that were not presented as discrete options in the human survey interface, and ensures every retained question is compatible with both `KevPointerModel` and `LetterModel` (which caps option count at 26 letters). Finally, 10 `QuestionID`s (`QID268` to `QID272` and `QID275` to `QID279`) are reused across two different survey blocks with different question types, so catalog lookup and question identifiers must be keyed by the expanded `csv_columns` (or `(QuestionID, BlockName)`) rather than `QuestionID` alone.
