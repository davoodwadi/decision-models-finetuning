# Persona versus question training dynamics and validation design (`train-grouped-diversevalid-800m`)

This document serves as the reference on validation split design and the statistical and optimization mechanics governing respondent-grouped training ([training/trainer.py](./training/trainer.py)) versus flat i.i.d. training ([training/iid_trainer.py](./training/iid_trainer.py)) across [train-grouped-diversevalid-grouped-800m.ipynb](./train-grouped-diversevalid-grouped-800m.ipynb) and [train-grouped-diversevalid-800m.ipynb](./train-grouped-diversevalid-800m.ipynb).

## Replica-filtered diverse validation split (`valid_diverse80`)

In the original `newvalid` setup ([train-grouped-newvalid-800m.ipynb](./train-grouped-newvalid-800m.ipynb)), validation held out all human responses to only 18 questions, of which 11 belonged to a single Twin-2K cereal battery (`QID9_089` to `QID9_099`). Because `group_train_records` in [data/base.py](./data/base.py) sorted each respondent's questions by exact character length, contiguous blocks of identical-length battery items (`QID9_001` to `QID9_088`) formed pure-topic training steps, producing severe sawtooth oscillations in validation loss whenever the training loop encountered or left `QID9`.

To make validation representative of the full survey distribution without leaking question marginals or slowing evaluation, `valid_diverse80` holds out $k = 80$ whole questions stratified across all 10 ISSP waves (four questions per year from 2015 to 2024) and 40 distinct Twin-2K question families, subsamples up to $m = 100$ respondents per held-out question (`8,000` validation records across `6,348` unique persona states), and removes near-duplicate training questions above a cosine similarity threshold of 0.90 to 0.95 via sentence embeddings (`1,764,410` training records across `1,510` questions). Empirically, switching from `valid_new` to `valid_diverse80` reduces detrended step-to-step validation residual noise by 3.5x (from 0.0341 to 0.0096) and cuts worst-case 50-step validation spikes by 4.4x (from +0.0919 to +0.0211).

## Controlled comparison of grouped versus i.i.d. training on Grummon

Comparing [train-grouped-diversevalid-grouped-800m.ipynb](./train-grouped-diversevalid-grouped-800m.ipynb) (`GroupedTrainer`, `shared_prefix=True`, `max_questions_per_group=32`, `batch_size=1`, `grad_accum_steps=12`, averaging `309.2` questions across `12` respondents per step) directly against [train-grouped-diversevalid-800m.ipynb](./train-grouped-diversevalid-800m.ipynb) (`IIDTrainer`, `shared_prefix=False`, `batch_size=12`, `grad_accum_steps=26`, averaging `312` random `(persona, question)` pairs across roughly `303` respondents per step) isolates the effect of respondent grouping while holding fixed the training set, `valid_diverse80` split, `jaredpalmer/kev-0.8b` initialization (`valid_diverse80 = 2.7238`, `grummon_kl = 2.0224`, `OLS MAPE = 104.13%`), LoRA rank (`64`, `lora_alpha=128`, `lora_dropout=0.05`), peak learning rate (`5e-5` under `OneCycleLR` with `pct_start=0.1`), and `seed=0`. While `IIDTrainer` optimizes in-distribution `valid_diverse80` slightly further (`1.8330` vs. `1.8432`), `GroupedTrainer` achieves roughly `0.10` bits lower mean KL divergence and `8.6` percentage points lower mean RCT treatment-effect MAPE on out-of-distribution Grummon, while running roughly 3.3x faster per epoch.

Across the controlled `diversevalid` runs, the 75th percentile of `grummon_kl` under `diversevalid-grouped` (`1.715`) lies well below the 25th percentile under both `diversevalid-iid` (`1.770`) and `diversevalid-iid-2epochs` (`1.736`). Moreover, out-of-distribution `grummon_kl` improves monotonically with the number of questions grouped per respondent per step (`1.6347` for `max_q=64`, `1.6856` for `max_q=32`, and `1.7865` for `max_q=1`).

## Why respondent grouping generalizes better on unseen questions

Evaluating the saved checkpoints across all 36 Grummon RCT questions shows that over 85 percent of `IIDTrainer`'s degradation relative to `GroupedTrainer` (`0.204` of `0.231` bits on a stratified 36-question slice) comes from out-of-distribution logit overconfidence on Grummon's unseen `option_descriptions` and unseen demographic attributes (`logit std = 0.486` with a `0.262`-bit temperature penalty under `IID best`, compared to `logit std = 0.294` and a `0.058`-bit penalty under `Grouped best`).

In `GroupedTrainer` (`shared_prefix=True`, [kev/kev/shared_prefix.py](./kev/kev/shared_prefix.py)), each of the `12` respondents in a step encodes a single shared state $h_s(\text{persona}_i)$ (one attribute ordering in `make_grouped_view`, one `lora_dropout=0.05` mask) and averages gradients across $M_i \approx 26$ to $64$ questions onto that state before multiplying the prefix Jacobian. Idiosyncratic single-question one-hot noise cancels by roughly $1/\sqrt{M_i}$ per respondent, turning each respondent's update from a noisy single `(persona, question)` one-hot draw into a denoised cross-question persona marginal, whereas `IIDTrainer` applies noisy single-draw one-hot gradients onto independently shuffled and dropped-out prefixes.

Although both trainers sum over the same `1.76M` records per epoch (`~312` questions per step), grouping trades respondent count per step (`12` vs. `~303`) for within-persona question count (`~26` vs. `1`), which interacts nonlinearly with AdamW (`m_t / sqrt(v_t)`). Writing each per-record gradient coordinate as $g_{i,q} = \mu + \alpha_i + \varepsilon_{i,q}$ (mean $\mu$, respondent and battery response-style effect $\alpha_i$, and cell one-hot noise $\varepsilon_{i,q}$), step variance shifts from $\sigma_\alpha^2 / 303 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2 / 312$ in `IIDTrainer` to $\sigma_\alpha^2 / 12 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2 / 312$ in `GroupedTrainer`. In `IIDTrainer`, averaging `~303` respondents per step keeps population marginal and Likert scale gradients smooth, yielding small $\sqrt{\hat{v}_t}$ and aggressive readout sharpening in late layers. In `GroupedTrainer`, `1.20x` to `1.31x` larger $\sqrt{\hat{v}_t}$ on `PointerHead` ([kev/kev/model.py](./kev/kev/model.py)) selectively damps late-layer (`L18` to `L23`, `0.85x` update norm vs. IID, cosine `0.39`) and readout-head (`0.82x` to `0.88x` update norm) drift while preserving full update magnitude (`0.99x` to `1.02x`) in early persona-encoding layers (`L0` to `L5`).

In practice, validation and training batch structure should remain decoupled, pairing `valid_diverse80` for low-variance validation monitoring with `GroupedTrainer` (`shared_prefix=True`, larger `max_questions_per_group` such as `64` as VRAM permits, optional `group_train_records_mixed` in [data/base.py](./data/base.py) to mix question batteries within each group, and optionally a lower learning rate on `model.net.head`) to preserve persona learning while preventing readout overconfidence.
