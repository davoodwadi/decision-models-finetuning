# Theory and design for grouped k-marginal training (`train-diversevalid-marginals-800m`)

This document presents the statistical theory, optimization dynamics, and implementation design for [train-diversevalid-marginals-800m.ipynb](../train-notebooks/train-diversevalid-marginals-800m.ipynb), preserving and augmenting the persona-grouped regime analyzed in [train-grouped-diversevalid-800m.md](./train-grouped-diversevalid-800m.md) by supervising exact $k$-trait empirical choice distributions across each unique persona's own answered questions.

## Persona-grouped marginal targets and Rao-Blackwell variance reduction

Unlike deterministic tasks such as code generation or mathematical reasoning, a survey persona $x_S$ specified by $k$ demographic or attitudinal traits governs an irreducible conditional choice distribution $P(Y \mid X_S = x_S)$ across human respondents sharing those traits. Training exclusively on full-persona (`13` to `15` trait) one-hot draws never directly supervises the coarse-to-fine conditional distributions $P(Y \mid X_S)$ for $k = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ traits, whereas our earlier flat `Stage 1` and `Stage 2` marginal runs stopped at $k \le 2$ under single-question i.i.d. updates (`batch_size = 8`), over-exposing the `1,510` training question stems and causing additive logit stacking when evaluated on `10` to `15` traits at test time (`1.886` and `1.863` on Grummon 2025 vs. `1.604` for respondent-grouped training).

To preserve the structural advantages of grouping without sampling artificial cross-survey question combinations or drawing personas with replacement, each epoch starts from the exact `68,477` baseline groups produced by `group_train_records` in [data/base.py](../data/base.py) (`1,764,410` records across `16,338` unique full personas when `include_country = False`, comprising `14,285` ISSP personas with `15` observed traits and roughly `2` groups each, plus `2,053` Twin-2K personas with `13` observed traits and roughly `20` groups each, yielding `5,707` steps at `grad_accum_steps = 12`). Note that `max_chars_per_group = 20,000` bounds the padded rectangle `len(group) * max_chars` because `DecisionModel.forward_rows_batch` in [kev/kev/model.py](../kev/kev/model.py) pads all $Q$ question branches of a shared-prefix hybrid pass to `[Q, max_branch_len]`, while `KevEncoder.prepare` in [models/kev_pointer.py](../models/kev_pointer.py) enforces `max_state = 65_536`, `max_branch = 73_728`, and `strict = True` with zero token truncation.

For a baseline group belonging to a unique persona $i$ with $D_i$ observed traits and any non-empty subset $S \subseteq \{1, \dots, D_i\}$ of size $|S| = k$ (`subpersona`), let $M_S(q)$ denote all rows in `train_df` that match $x_{i, S}$ on every trait in $S$ and answered question $q$ from that group, with sample count $n_S(q) = |M_S(q)| \ge 1$. Rather than sequential tree shrinkage (which introduces trait-order bias, double-counts nested subgroups, and violates marginal consistency across sibling categories), each question $q$ in the group receives the exact unsmoothed empirical marginal distribution

$$\bar{p}_S(q) = \frac{\text{vote\_counts}_S(q)}{n_S(q)} = \frac{1}{n_S(q)} \sum_{r \in M_S(q)} y_{r, q}.$$

Because $M_S(q)$ is a plain set intersection, $\bar{p}_S(q)$ is invariant to trait ordering, weights every matching observation uniformly by $1 / n_S(q)$ with zero double-counting, sums to `1.0` identically, and satisfies the Law of Total Probability across every partition of an additional trait. Because cross-entropy loss $-\sum_o t_o \log \hat{p}_\theta(x_S, q)_o$ and its logit gradient $\hat{p}_\theta(x_S, q) - t$ are linear in the target $t$, conditional on $X_S = x_{i, S}$ we have

$$\mathbb{E}\big[\bar{p}_S(q) \mid X_S = x_{i, S}\big] = P(Y \mid X_S = x_{i, S}), \qquad \text{Var}\big(\bar{p}_S(q) \mid X_S = x_{i, S}\big) = \frac{P(Y \mid X_S = x_{i, S})\big(1 - P(Y \mid X_S = x_{i, S})\big)}{n_S(q)}$$

for every $n_S(q) \ge 1$. At small $k$, averaging $n_S(q)$ respondents Rao-Blackwellizes the target, shrinking single-draw variance by $1 / n_S(q)$ and driving the expected gradient $\hat{p}_\theta - \bar{p}_S$ to zero at the true conditional entropy. At $k = D_i$, where $n_S(q)$ approaches `1`, $\bar{p}_S(q)$ smoothly recovers the full-persona respondent-grouped baseline (`diversevalid-grouped`).

## Why stationary mixed-k over the informative ladder is optimal

Comparing candidate schedules mathematically under `OneCycleLR` ($\eta_t \to 0$) shows why mixing $k$ stationarily inside every optimizer step strictly outperforms a sequential stage curriculum ($k = 1 \to k = 2 \to \dots \to k = D_i$). Under sequential stages, decaying step sizes converge toward a stationary point of the late-stage objective; because `84%` to `99.4%` of groups at $k \ge 8$ reduce to $n_S(q) = 1$ one-hot targets, finishing on large $k$ relaxes the coarse marginal constraints $\hat{p}_\theta(x_S, q) \approx \bar{p}_S(q)$ and erases early conditional-entropy calibration. Mixing $k$ across the 12 groups in every step instead minimizes the joint coarse-to-fine risk

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \sum_{k} \pi(k)\,\mathbb{E}_{|S| = k}\Big[D_{\text{KL}}\big(\bar{p}_S(q) \,\|\, \hat{p}_\theta(x_S, q)\big)\Big]$$

all the way to $\eta_T \to 0$, anchoring question intercepts and low-order effects on high-$n_S$ soft targets while simultaneously forcing the shared state $h_s(x_S)$ on full $D_i$-trait prompts in the same step to normalize sub-additively rather than stacking single-trait log-odds at test time.

Expanding every baseline group across all $k = 1, \dots, D_i$ (`~14x` expansion to `~950,000` groups and `~79,000` steps) is likewise suboptimal for two independent reasons. First, the number of unique training question stems is fixed at $Q = 1,510$, so `14x` passes over-expose training stems and inflate readout memorization on unseen out-of-distribution questions such as Grummon 2025 (`36` unseen questions, where `diversevalid-grouped` already peaks between `1,800` and `3,000` steps). Second, letting $k^*_i = \min\{k : n_{S_k}(q) = 1\}$ denote the prefix length at which persona $i$ becomes unique (`median` $k^* \approx 6$ to $7$), every $k \in \{k^*_i, \dots, D_i\}$ yields the exact same one-hot target $\bar{p}_{S_k}(q) \equiv y_{i, q}$ with unit residual correlation $\text{Corr}(\varepsilon_{S_k}, \varepsilon_{S_{k'}}) = 1$, repeating single-respondent noise eight to nine times with zero variance reduction.

Crucially, nested $k$ groups derived from the same persona must also be shuffled across optimizer steps rather than packed into the same 12-group step. Packing multiple $k$ views of one persona and its 32 questions into a single step collapses independent personas per step from $R = 12$ toward $R \approx 1$ to $2$, inflating persona-direction step variance $\sigma_\alpha^2 / R$ by `6x` to `12x` and repeating identical question stems inside one AdamW update. Shuffling augmented groups across the epoch maintains $R = 12$ independent personas times roughly `26` distinct questions per step, preserving the exact AdamW `PointerHead` second-moment damping $\sqrt{\hat{v}_t}$ established in [train-grouped-diversevalid-800m.md](./train-grouped-diversevalid-800m.md).

Finally, the empirical table above shows why a uniform prior $k \sim \text{Uniform}\{1, \dots, D_i\}$ is inefficient, spending over half of each epoch (`k = 7` to `15`) on the $n_S = 1$ one-hot plateau while allocating only `~7%` of groups to full test-length personas ($k = D_i$) and `~35%` to genuine multi-respondent marginals ($k \le 5$). Under a functional ANOVA decomposition, concentrating $\pi(k)$ on the informative ladder $k \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, D_i\}$ (e.g., allocating roughly `25%` to `30%` of groups to full $k = D_i$ and `70%` to `75%` across nested prefixes of a per-persona trait order $(a_1, \dots, a_{D_i})$ at $k \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$, skipping the redundant $n_S = 1$ plateau at $k = 7, \dots, D_i - 1$ within a `1x` to `2x` epoch budget) maximizes Rao-Blackwell denoising on low-order main and interaction effects while maintaining strong direct coverage of full `13` to `15` trait test prompts.

## Boolean-mask marginal lookup and controlled training setup

Rather than integer-encoding cohorts or re-scanning `train_df` per group, exact marginals over `train_df` are computed via two transparent structures built once in `~1.5` seconds: a dictionary `trait_masks[(col, val)]` of `16,338`-element boolean arrays over the deduplicated full personas, and per-question arrays `qid_persona_ids[qid]` and `qid_vote_counts[qid]` storing the unique persona indices that answered `qid` and their summed one-hot votes. For each baseline group, intersecting `trait_masks[(col, val)]` across `subpersona` takes a few microseconds in L1 cache, indexing `qid_persona_ids[qid]` sums `vote_counts` and `matching_n` across the group's questions, and formatting via `make_grouped_view` streams all `68,477` groups (`5,707` steps, `1,764,410` questions) in `21` seconds (`3.0 ms/step` on CPU). No changes are required inside `GroupedTrainer.train_one_step` in [training/trainer.py](../training/trainer.py), which already evaluates exact soft-target cross-entropy `-(t * logp).sum()` and forward KL divergence $D_{\text{KL}}(\bar{p}_S \| \hat{p}_\theta)$ in bits on arbitrary probability distributions `q["target"]` under `shared_prefix = True`.

In [train-diversevalid-marginals-800m.ipynb](../train-notebooks/train-diversevalid-marginals-800m.ipynb), Cell 0 anchors the working directory to `code/` so relative imports, `.cache/`, and `runs/` paths match the baseline notebooks, holding all split, model, and optimizer settings fixed to `diversevalid-grouped` (`INCLUDE_COUNTRY = False`, `VALID_SEED = 0`, `valid_diverse80` at `2.7238` and `grummon2025` at `2.0224` at step 0, `jaredpalmer/kev-0.8b`, `lora_rank = 64`, `lora_alpha = 128`, `lora_dropout = 0.05`, `lr = 5e-5` with `OneCycleLR` and `pct_start = 0.1`, `batch_size = 1`, `grad_accum_steps = 12`, `max_questions_per_group = 32`, `val_every = [50, 200]`, and `save_dir = "runs/kev-0.8b-resp-issp+twin2k-diversevalid-marginals"`).

## Stateless marginal lookup and trainer subclass architecture

The codebase integration keeps [data/base.py](../data/base.py), [training/trainer.py](../training/trainer.py), and all existing training notebooks untouched by isolating the new regime into two small modules. First, `code/data/marginals.py` provides `build_marginal_lookup`, `augment_group_with_marginals`, and `build_marginal_epoch_groups`. Given `train_df`, `build_marginal_lookup` validates the required survey and persona columns fail-fast and precomputes `PersonaMarginalLookup` containing `trait_masks`, `qid_persona_ids`, and `qid_vote_counts`. At the start of each epoch, `build_marginal_epoch_groups` calls `group_train_records` to obtain the shuffled `68,477` unique-persona baseline groups without replacement, draws a stateless granularity `k_choice = rng.choice(k_schedule)` from `k_schedule = (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, "full", "full")` for each group (`k_traits = len(full_persona)` when `k_choice == "full"` and `min(k_choice, len(full_persona))` otherwise), samples `k_traits` observed attributes from `full_persona` to form `subpersona`, and calls `augment_group_with_marginals` to intersect `trait_masks` and replace each question's `human_dist` with `vote_counts / matching_n`.

Second, `code/training/marginal_trainer.py` defines `MarginalGroupedTrainer` as a thin subclass of `GroupedTrainer` (re-exported in [training/__init__.py](../training/__init__.py)). Because `build_marginal_epoch_groups` preserves the exact `68,477` group count and `5,707` step schedule of `group_train_records` and deterministic epoch seeding via `f"{self.seed}:epoch:{epoch_idx}"`, neither `GroupedTrainer.train` nor `GroupedTrainer.train_one_step` requires any modification. In `train_one_step`, `make_grouped_view` already formats the shared `subpersona` prefix in randomized attribute order and converts arbitrary soft `human_dist` vectors into `q["target"]` tensors, supervising exact soft cross-entropy `-(t * logp).sum()` and logging exact forward KL divergence $D_{\text{KL}}(\bar{p}_S \| \hat{p}_\theta)$ in bits, while mid-epoch `resume = True` deterministically reconstructs the identical augmented `epoch_groups` and resumes from `next_start`. Accordingly, `MarginalGroupedTrainer` overrides only `_epoch_groups_for` to return the marginal-augmented groups, `_run_signature` to record `"trainer": "marginal_grouped"` and `"k_schedule"` for fail-fast checkpoint verification, and `describe` to display the marginal schedule and unique-persona counts in the configuration summary.
